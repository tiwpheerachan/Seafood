# About Ra Beung Lay Seafood — Copy & Voice Reference

> Use this for the homepage hero, the /the-beach page, and any "story" sections. Voice is warm, direct, generous — not premium or corporate.

---

## Brand essence (one paragraph)

Ra Beung Lay Seafood is a beachfront, family-run Thai seafood restaurant on Aqua Beach near Ao Nang, Krabi. The name means "sea veranda" — and the place lives up to it: long wooden tables, bamboo and thatched roofing, low Thai-style mat seating, all opening directly onto the water with the famous Krabi karst formations on the horizon. The food is honest, generous, and rooted in southern Thai tradition — fresh fish, steamed crab and mussels, spicy curries, and signature dishes like Khao Yum and Massaman that you won't find done as well elsewhere on the strip. With over 3,000 five-star Google reviews, Ra Beung Lay has become the seafood spot tourists pass on to friends — but until now, it's lived almost entirely on Facebook and TripAdvisor. This site is where it lives next.

## Suggested /about page narrative (~250 words)

Ra Beung Lay opened with one idea: cook the seafood that arrives at the back door, in the place where you can see the sea it came from. That's still how it works. The boats land at Ao Nam Mao in the morning. By midday the kitchen is moving. The customers — most days, half tourists and half regulars from Krabi Town — sit on long communal tables under thatched roofs, or kick their shoes off and take the low platforms with cushions, and order more than they should.

The menu doesn't try to be everything. It's southern Thai, with the dishes you'd hope a place this good would do well: deep-fried whole fish with three-flavor sauce, steamed mussels with seafood dipping sauce, tom yum with whatever was best at market, and the curries that make this part of Thailand worth flying to — Massaman, spicy shrimp, the rice salad called Khao Yum that tastes like nowhere else.

Ra Beung Lay isn't fine dining. It isn't trying to be. It's the kind of restaurant that earns three thousand five-star reviews because it does the simple things — fresh seafood, generous portions, a view that does the rest of the work — without any of the fuss.

If you're in Ao Nang, walk down the beach. If you're in Krabi Town, the tuk-tuk's worth it. If you've already been, you know what to order.

ระเบียงเล ซีฟู้ด — see you on the veranda.

## Voice & tone guidelines

| Trait              | Show this                                                | Avoid this                              |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Warmth             | "See you on the veranda."                                | "We hope you'll join us soon."          |
| Generosity         | "Order more than you should."                            | "Refined portions of premium seafood."  |
| Specificity        | "Massaman, spicy shrimp, the rice salad called Khao Yum" | "Authentic Thai flavors."               |
| Rootedness         | "The boats land at Ao Nam Mao in the morning."           | "Sourced from local suppliers."         |
| Confidence         | "If you've already been, you know what to order."        | "Try our award-winning dishes."         |
| Bilingual respect  | Mix Thai phrases naturally: "ระเบียงเล" alongside English. | Forced romanization without context.    |

## Phrases that already exist in their world (use them)

- "ระเบียงเล" / "ระเบียงเล ซีฟู้ด"
- "Ra-Beung-Lay Seafood"
- "Aqua Beach" / "หาดอควา"

## Phrases to NOT invent

- Do not fabricate a founding year, founder name, or founder story. We don't have those facts.
- Do not invent specific dish origin claims ("the chef trained in...", "the recipe was passed down...") — verify or omit.
- Do not name suppliers, fishermen, or family members — verify before naming people.
- Do not claim awards ("Krabi's #1", "Lonely Planet pick", etc.) without verifying.

## Bilingual approach for the site

- All headlines should appear in **English with Thai subheading** OR have a TH/EN toggle.
- Menu items should always show **both Thai and English names** side-by-side. International tourists love seeing the Thai script. Thai customers need it for clarity.
- Practical info (address, hours, phone) should be **bilingual by default** since Thai-speaking customers may not parse English-formatted addresses.
- "About" copy can be English-led; provide a Thai version as `about-th.md` and have the owner approve the translation.
